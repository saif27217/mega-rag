### 🧬 Biochem Perspective

Based on the provided textbook sources, elevated creatine kinase (CK) in athletes is primarily caused by intensive physical activity and muscle adaptation:

- **Intensive exercise and muscle damage** – "Intensive exercise is associated with transient elevations of cardiac biomarkers, markers of muscle damage, enhanced platelet aggregation..." [Source 3, p.130]. This includes elevations of creatine kinase (CK) and its MB isoenzyme.
- **High training volume (>12 hours/week)** – "Concentrations of creatine kinase (CK), creatine kinase MB (CK-MB), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), and lactate dehydrogenase (LD) are increased in individuals where physical activity is greater than 12 hours per week" [Source 3, p.130]. This effect is prolonged, as "in individuals who are physically active more than 12 hours per week, concentrations of creatine kinase (CK), Creatine kinase MB (CK-MB), ALT, and LD are increased for a prolonged period of time" [Source 4, p.145].
- **Unaccustomed or strenuous exercise** – "Exercise, particularly if unaccustomed, and muscle trauma can increase serum CK activity, which may ascend as high as 10 times the URL within 24 hours of activity" [Source 6, p.832].
- **Skeletal muscle adaptation** – In runners specifically, "increase in serum CK-MB in runners, for example, may be related to the adaptation by the skeletal muscle during regular training" [Source 5, p.84].

These changes are transient for most markers (returning to baseline within 3 hours), but "in professional sportsmen (eg, marathon runners), a large proportion of laboratory results may fall outside the usual reference intervals" [Source 3, p.130; also Source 4, p.145].
